Research case 01 · Team abc123 · Algothon 2026

Forecasting under a limited effective sample

Why a slightly higher backtest was not enough to replace a simpler submission.

The question was direction, then evidence.

Algothon’s published objective was mean P&L multiplied by SR² / (SR² + 1). The team began by examining that objective and its trading constraints, before expanding the model search. In its analysis, position caps fixed the sizing decision and the commission ceiling was small relative to the simulated book’s mean daily P&L. The research effort therefore concentrated on the direction of the next position. These conclusions belonged to the competition’s particular rules, not to trading in general. [1]

The subsequent decision was harder: when several closely related candidates look better on the same historical sample, how much improvement is enough to justify changing the submission?

A large panel was still one market history.

The development panel contained 1,500 days across 51 assets. Multiplying the two gives 76,500 asset-day cells, but does not make them independent observations. The published analysis attributes roughly 22% of the correlation trace to one common factor and estimates about five effective cross-sectional units against a nominal fifty. The 500 hidden evaluation days were a separate test window. [1] [2]

That dependence shaped the standard of evidence. A small score gain on the development panel could reflect the particular market history or selection among many candidates, rather than a reliably better forecasting rule.

Price selection luck before selecting a model.

The team’s published record estimates a scored block’s standard error at approximately 131–143 points and the score benefit of selecting the best of a few hundred zero-skill variants at approximately 166 points. Seven nearby candidate variants exceeded the chosen submission’s nominal development score, but the largest reported margin was only 1.14 points. None of those seven variants replaced the submission. [1]

The frozen final code is described as a causal online ensemble with a seven-feature, non-negative logistic blend. The relevant design choice was to retain the simpler, better-understood structure when the available evidence did not establish that a more elaborate variant was better. The submitted code and the earlier engine are both preserved, so the account can be checked against the actual implementations. [3] [4]

The 984.62 result belongs to the predecessor, not the final submission. The two scores are not a like-for-like model comparison. The research notes report a public-data replay mean of 1,144.89 for the final engine and a hidden-window result of about 1,085.17; the team interprets that gap in light of its previously estimated block uncertainty. This page reports that interpretation without turning a single hidden window into a general performance claim. [1] [2]

What the case does and does not establish.

The evidence supports a research process: derive implications from the objective, account for dependence, and set a standard for accepting apparent improvements. Competition rules, position limits and a particular development history bounded the experiment. Hidden evaluation reduces reuse of the development sample, but one 500-day window does not establish persistence across market regimes. The score is a competition statistic, not an investment return.

All figures here come from the team’s already-public record. The competition harness was not rerun for this webpage, and no private datasets or unpublished experiments were added. The original 69-page report remains the detailed source for derivations, evidence grading and the falsification record. [5]
